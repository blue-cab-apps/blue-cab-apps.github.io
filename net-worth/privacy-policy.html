Net Worth Privacy Policy

Effective date: 8 October 2026

The short version

  • No account, no sign-up. You never give us your name, email or anything else to use Net Worth.
  • We never see your financial information. What you enter stays on your iPhone and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud storage. Apple hosts that storage and we have no access to it.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no third-party code. The app has no server of its own and sends nothing to us.
  • Premium is sold and billed by Apple. We never see your payment details.

Who we are

Net Worth is an iPhone app for keeping track of what you own, what you owe, and your net worth over time. It is made and operated by Emmanuel Tan (“we”, “us”), the developer listed as the seller of Net Worth on the App Store. This policy explains what happens to information in the app.

The information you enter, and where it lives

The only information in Net Worth is what you type into it:

  • your accounts: a name, a category and a balance for each thing you own (cash, investments, property and so on) and each thing you owe (credit cards, loans, a mortgage and so on);
  • your history: each day you update a balance, the app saves that day’s totals so it can draw your net worth over time;
  • past estimates you choose to add (roughly what your net worth was in an earlier month);
  • your goals.

Net Worth does not connect to your bank or any other financial service. It never asks for your name, email address, location, contacts or photos.

That information is kept in two places, and only two:

  1. On your iPhone, in the app’s own storage.
  2. In your private iCloud database, if your iPhone is signed in to iCloud. The app uses Apple’s iCloud (CloudKit) to keep a copy in the private database of your iCloud account, so it appears on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account and survives losing or replacing your phone. That database belongs to your iCloud account. Apple stores it, under Apple’s own iCloud terms and privacy policy, and we cannot open it, read it or download it.

If your iPhone is not signed in to iCloud, everything stays on that iPhone only. The app works fully either way.

Net Worth has no server of its own. It never sends your information to us or to anyone else. The only connections it relies on are the ones iOS makes on its behalf to iCloud and the App Store.

What we can and cannot see

We cannot see your financial information, ever. Your balances, account names, history, estimates, goals and net worth never reach a server we control, because there isn’t one.

We do not receive your name, email address, Apple Account details, device identifiers, location or any record of how you use the app.

What we may see comes from Apple, in anonymous or aggregate form:

  • App Store sales and subscription reports (for example, how many subscriptions were sold in a country). These contain no payment details and cannot be linked to anything you entered in the app.
  • Crash reports and app usage statistics, only if you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in iOS Settings (Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements). Apple provides these without identifying you. They describe how the app behaved (for example, where it crashed), not what you entered in it.

If you email us, we see your email address and whatever you write, and we use it only to reply to you.

Small settings the app keeps on your iPhone

Apart from your own entries, the app remembers a few settings so it behaves the same way each time you open it:

  • whether you’ve finished first-time setup;
  • whether Hide Balances is turned on;
  • whether you’ve said “Not now” to a suggested goal, or to the card suggesting you sign in to iCloud;
  • when your two months of included Premium began, and whether the app has already told you about their start and end.

These stay in the app’s local settings on your iPhone. One of them, the date your included Premium months began, is also saved in your own iCloud account’s key-value storage (private to your Apple Account, like the rest of your iCloud data), so that reinstalling the app or adding a second device doesn’t change when those months end. It holds only that date.

Notifications. If you allow notifications, the app schedules two kinds of local reminder on your iPhone: the update reminder you choose (weekly, every two weeks, monthly, on payday, or never, on the day and at the time you pick), and one notice three days before your included Premium months end. iOS schedules and shows them on your device. Nothing is sent to us. The app asks for notification permission only when you choose a reminder cadence, at the end of setup or later in Settings.

Premium subscriptions

Net Worth is free to use. Premium is an optional, auto-renewing subscription at $2.99 per month or $34.99 per year (US prices; the App Store shows the price in your currency before you confirm). Every new user gets 2 months of Premium included at no charge. That is not a purchase: no payment details are asked for and nothing renews.

Purchases, billing, renewals, cancellations and refunds are handled entirely by Apple through your Apple Account. We never receive your payment card or billing details. To decide which features to unlock, the app asks Apple’s in-app purchase system whether your Apple Account has an active Net Worth subscription. That check happens between your iPhone and Apple.

You can change or cancel Premium at any time in the app (Settings → Premium → Manage Subscription) or in iOS Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions.

Deleting your data

  • Erase All Data (in the app’s Settings → Your data) permanently deletes every account, update, estimate and goal from your iPhone and from your private iCloud database, so they also disappear from your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. This can’t be undone. The app then starts again from first-time setup. It keeps only the start date of your included Premium months and whether you’ve been told about them (so erasing doesn’t restart them), and your Hide Balances setting. Erasing does not cancel a subscription; manage that separately, as described above.
  • Deleting the app removes the copy and settings stored on that iPhone. Anything already saved to iCloud stays in your iCloud account until you erase it from the app or remove it yourself.
  • Removing the app’s iCloud data entirely is done in iOS itself, not in the app: open Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage (the name varies slightly between iOS versions), choose Net Worth, and delete its data.

We hold no copy of your data and there is no Net Worth account, so there is nothing for us to delete on our side and no account to close.

Security

On your iPhone, your data is protected by iOS and your device passcode. In iCloud, it is protected by Apple’s iCloud security. We recommend using a device passcode. Hide Balances blurs amounts on the Dashboard for moments when someone might be looking over your shoulder. It changes only what’s shown on screen and is not a security feature.

Children

Net Worth is not directed at children under 13, or under the higher minimum age that applies where you live (16 in some countries). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. In fact, the app collects none from anyone.

Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live (for example, in the EU, the UK or California), you may have the right to access, correct, delete or take a copy of the personal information a business holds about you. We don’t hold any. Everything is in your hands directly: you can see and change it all in the app, erase it from the app, and manage your iCloud storage through Apple. We do not sell or share personal information. If you have a question about your rights, contact us at the address below.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the effective date at the top. If a change is significant, we’ll also mention it in the App Store release notes for the update that brings it. If Net Worth ever starts collecting any information, this policy and the app’s App Store privacy details will be updated before that happens.
